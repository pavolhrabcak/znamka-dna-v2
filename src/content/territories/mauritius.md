Mauritius in the Indian Ocean is home to the world's most famous stamp error — the 1847 "Post Office" Mauritius, on which the words "Post Paid" were mistakenly inscribed as "Post Office." Only 27 examples survive and they command the highest prices at auction of any British colonial stamps.

Britain captured Mauritius from France in 1810. The island economy was based on sugar production using indentured labour after the abolition of slavery. Later pictorial definitives from De La Rue and Waterlow & Sons depict the island's scenic coastline and economic activities.
