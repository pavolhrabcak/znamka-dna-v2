Aden was a British Crown Colony occupying a small peninsula on the southwestern tip of the Arabian Peninsula, at the mouth of the Red Sea. The British East India Company seized Aden in 1839 to suppress piracy and secure the sea route to India. Its natural deep-water harbour made it one of the busiest coaling and trading ports in the world.

Aden became a Crown Colony in 1937, separated from the wider Aden Protectorate. Its stamp issues are distinct from those of the surrounding protectorate states.

The first distinctive stamps for Aden were issued in 1937, engraved and printed by Recess. Designs featured the dhow — the traditional Arab sailing vessel central to Aden's trade — alongside views of the harbour and the Crater district.
