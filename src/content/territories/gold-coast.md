The Gold Coast on the Gulf of Guinea in West Africa was named for its rich gold deposits. Britain established forts along the coast from the seventeenth century, and the territory became a Crown Colony in 1874. The Gold Coast was the first British sub-Saharan African territory to gain independence, becoming Ghana in 1957.

The KGV pictorial definitives of 1928, produced by Waterlow & Sons, are among the most beautiful of West African issues, featuring Christiansborg Castle and local industry. The subsequent KGVI and QEII series maintained the high standard of recess engraving.
