British Honduras on the Caribbean coast of Central America was a Crown Colony from 1862 until it was renamed Belize in 1973, achieving full independence in 1981. The territory's economy relied on the timber trade — mahogany and logwood — which features prominently in its stamp designs.

The KGVI definitives of 1938, engraved by Waterlow & Sons, depict scenes of the mahogany industry with characteristic fine-line recess engraving that captures the tropical forest settings in rich detail.
