The Turks and Caicos Islands, at the southeastern end of the Bahamas archipelago, built their colonial economy on the production of sea salt. The salt raking industry is depicted on many of the islands' most characteristic stamp designs.

The long-running KGVI definitive series of 1938, engraved by De La Rue, forms one of the most complete and varied sets of any small British colony. The depiction of salt-raking — figures working on the gleaming salt flats — is one of the most distinctive motifs in British colonial philately.
