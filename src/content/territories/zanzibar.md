Zanzibar, an archipelago off the coast of East Africa, became a British protectorate in 1890. The island had been the centre of the East African slave trade and the Arab spice trade. Cloves, nutmeg and cinnamon are reflected in its colonial imagery.

Zanzibar's stamps, produced throughout the colonial period by De La Rue, are notable for their portrait series featuring the Sultan alongside British monarchs, reflecting the unusual dual sovereignty of the protectorate. The 1964 revolution ended the sultanate, and Zanzibar merged with Tanganyika to form Tanzania.
