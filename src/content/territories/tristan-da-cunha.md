Tristan da Cunha is the most remote inhabited island in the world, lying in the South Atlantic some 2,800 km from South Africa. Britain claimed the island in 1816, partly to prevent it being used as a base for rescuing Napoleon from nearby St Helena.

The island did not issue its own stamps until 1952, making it one of the last British colonial territories to do so. The first series, engraved and printed by De La Rue, depicts island scenes and local wildlife. These first issues are keenly collected given the island's romantic isolation.
