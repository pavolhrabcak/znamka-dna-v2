St Helena, a remote volcanic island in the South Atlantic, was discovered by the Portuguese in 1502 and became a British possession under the East India Company in 1659. The island is best known as the final place of exile for Napoleon Bonaparte, who died there in 1821.

The KGVI definitives of 1938, produced by Waterlow & Sons using recess engraving, depict the dramatic landscape of Jamestown and the island's historic buildings. St Helena's philatelic material is highly sought after by South Atlantic specialists.
