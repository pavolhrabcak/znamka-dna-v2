The Cayman Islands, three islands in the western Caribbean, were administered under Jamaica until 1962 when they chose to remain a British Crown Colony. The islands were known for seafaring, turtle fishing and, in the twentieth century, for financial services.

The KGVI definitives of 1938, printed by De La Rue, depict the colony's turtle industry and harbour scenes. The two-colour recess-engraved designs are among the most charming of the smaller Caribbean dependencies.
