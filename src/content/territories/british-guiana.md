British Guiana was a British colony on the north-eastern coast of South America — the only British territory on the South American mainland. It was formed from the former Dutch colonies of Essequibo, Demerara and Berbice, which Britain acquired at the Treaty of Paris in 1814.

The colony issued its own stamps from 1850, making it one of the earliest British colonial stamp-issuing territories. It is home to the world's most valuable stamp: the British Guiana 1c Magenta of 1856, a unique surviving example now worth tens of millions of dollars.

The 1934 Pictorial definitive series under KGV, engraved and printed by Waterlow & Sons, depicted economic activities of the colony: gold mining, sugar cane, rice cultivation, and scenes on the Demerara and Essequibo rivers. The quality of Waterlow's recess engraving is considered among the finest of the era.
