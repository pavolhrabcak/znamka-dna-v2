St. Lucia is an island in the Eastern Caribbean, alternating between British and French control before Britain gained permanent possession under the Treaty of Paris in 1814. It was administered as part of the Windward Islands group from 1838 to 1960.

The colony issued its own stamps from 1860. The KGVI pictorial definitive series of 1938–1948, printed by Bradbury, Wilkinson & Co., depicted economic activities of the island. The loading of bananas — St. Lucia's principal export — was among the most characteristic motifs of the series. St. Lucia gained independence in 1979 and remains a Commonwealth realm.
